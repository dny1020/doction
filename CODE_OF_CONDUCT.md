# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We want participating in doction to be a good use of your time, whoever you are. Everyone
involved — maintainers, contributors, people filing issues — is expected to treat the
others as colleagues.

## Expected behaviour

- Assume the other person is competent and acting in good faith.
- Critique the code, the design, or the argument. Not the person.
- Accept that "no" is a complete answer to a feature request, and that a maintainer
  declining scope is not hostility.
- Say what you actually mean, briefly. Directness is welcome; contempt is not.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, insults, or personal attacks, in issues, pull requests, or commit messages.
- Demeaning comments about someone's identity, background, or level of experience.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome sexual attention.
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission.
- Sustained disruption: reopening settled decisions to relitigate them, or demanding
  unpaid work from maintainers.

## Scope

This applies in every project space — issues, pull requests, discussions, commit
messages, and the security advisory channel — and to public behaviour where someone is
plausibly representing the project.

## Reporting

Report a problem privately by opening a
[security advisory](https://github.com/dny1020/doction/security/advisories/new), which is
a private channel to the maintainer, or by contacting the maintainer through their GitHub
profile. Reports are handled confidentially.

Do not report conduct problems in a public issue. It rarely helps the person reporting.

## Enforcement

The maintainer decides and acts. In rough order of escalation: a private word, a public
correction, editing or deleting the offending content, a temporary block, a permanent ban
from the project.

Enforcement is proportionate and does not require a formal process. A single-maintainer
project cannot offer an appeals board; what it can offer is that reports get read and
acted on.
